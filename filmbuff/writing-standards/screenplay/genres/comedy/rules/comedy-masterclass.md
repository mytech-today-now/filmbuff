## Subgenre Mastery

### Romantic Comedy (Rom-Com) Specifics
- **Engine**: Mutual misperception + escalating vulnerability.  
- **Rule**: The bigger the emotional wall, the bigger the final payoff. Never resolve attraction before Act 3.  
- **Key Beat**: The “grand gesture” must be earned through prior humiliation, not wish-fulfillment.  
- **Examples**: *When Harry Met Sally* (delicatessen scene), *Crazy, Stupid, Love* (yard makeover as character growth), *The Proposal* (Alaska family chaos).  
- **Pitfall**: Cute without conflict = saccharine. Fix by weaponizing opposing love languages.

### Dark Comedy
- **Engine**: Moral decay presented as normal.  
- **Rule**: The darker the deed, the lighter the tone must stay. Audience must root for flawed people doing bad things for understandable reasons.  
- **Examples**: *In Bruges* (hitmen therapy sessions), *Fargo* (woodchipper aftermath small talk), *Barry* (assassin support group).  
- **Technique**: Use mundane domestic details to normalize horror.

### Mockumentary / Found-Footage Comedy
- **Engine**: Camera as unreliable straight man.  
- **Rule**: Never break documentary logic even when absurdity peaks. Interviews must reveal character truth while characters lie.  
- **Examples**: *This Is Spinal Tap* (Stonehenge fiasco), *What We Do in the Shadows* (vampire council meetings), *The Office* (talking-head irony).  
- **Page Technique**: Heavy use of `[DIRECT TO CAMERA]` and interview intercuts.

### Cringe / Awkward Comedy
- **Engine**: Prolonged social violation with zero escape.  
- **Rule**: Extend discomfort 2–3 beats longer than feels safe, then release with unexpected empathy.  
- **Examples**: *Curb Your Enthusiasm* (social norm violations), *The Rehearsal* (Nathan Fielder), *I Think You Should Leave* (sketch escalation).

### Absurdist & Surreal Comedy
- **Engine**: Dream logic with emotional stakes.  
- **Rule**: Establish one impossible rule early and obey it religiously.  
- **Examples**: *Everything Everywhere All at Once* (hot dog fingers), *Monty Python’s Flying Circus* (Ministry of Silly Walks), *The Eric Andre Show* (interview destruction).

## Long-Form Rhythm & Pacing Architecture

**The Comedy Waveform**:  
Build micro-waves (scene level) inside macro-waves (act level).  
- **Rule**: Never have two high-energy comedic sequences back-to-back. Insert “valley” scenes of relative calm or character reflection.

**Pacing Grid** (per 110-page feature):  
- Pages 1-25: Establish baseline laugh rhythm  
- Pages 26-60: Rising wave — 20% density increase  
- Pages 61-85: Peak chaos wave  
- Pages 86-100: Descending wave with earned sincerity  
- Pages 101-110: Final button cascade  

**Running Gag Lifecycle**:  
1. Introduction (subtle)  
2. Reinforcement (2–3 variations)  
3. Mutation (context changes meaning)  
4. Apotheosis (biggest payoff)  
5. Inversion (final ironic twist)  

**Sequence Rhythm**: 7–12 minute comedic sequences maximum before a 2–4 minute breather.

## Ensemble Orchestration & Chemistry Engineering

**Comedy Energy Types** (assign to every character):  
- **Chaos Agent**: Creates disorder  
- **Order Enforcer**: Reacts with increasing hysteria  
- **Wildcard**: Unpredictable wildcard energy  
- **Anchor**: Grounds group with deadpan or sincerity  
- **Spark**: Catalyst who ignites others  

**Tag-Team Rule**: No joke should be completed by the same character who started it. Minimum three voices per major comedic sequence.  

**Chemistry Mapping**: Create a 5×5 grid of character pairs and pre-plan their unique comedic dynamic.

## Dialogue as Comedy Weaponry

**Advanced Techniques**:  
- **Interrupted Rhythm**: Cut characters off mid-thought for comic frustration.  
- **Echo Chamber**: Characters repeat each other’s words with escalating distortion.  
- **Status Shifts**: Rapid up/down status changes in conversation.  
- **Linguistic Idiosyncrasy**: Give every character a unique verbal tic that becomes comedic under pressure.  
- **Subtext Bomb**: A line that seems innocent but detonates later via callback.  

**Dialogue Density Rule**: In peak comedic scenes, target 60–70% joke-per-line ratio while maintaining natural flow.

## Comedic World-Building & Internal Logic Systems

**Logic Bible Creation**: Document every established rule of the comedic universe on page 1 of development.  
**Rule**: Any new rule introduced after page 30 must pay off by page 90 or be cut.  

**Absurdity Threshold**: Define exactly how far reality can bend before it breaks audience trust.  

**Environmental Comedy**: Locations must be active participants.

## Cultural Context, Sensitivity & Modern Relevance

- Punch up, not down — but “up” is contextual.  
- Self-deprecation from insider perspectives is gold.  
- Topical humor must be character-first.  
- Inclusive perspectives expand joke possibilities.  

**Technique**: Use “insider/outsider” dynamics for fresh angles on culture clash.

## Format-Specific Comedy Strategies

- **Feature Film**: High concept + emotional arc.  
- **TV Pilot**: Establish all running gags and world rules in first 8 minutes.  
- **TV Episode**: “A-story comedy + B-story heart” structure.  
- **Sketch**: One premise, three beats, button.  
- **Streaming Limited Series**: Slow-burn escalation across episodes.

## Advanced Testing & Iteration Frameworks

**Laugh Track Simulation**:  
1. Generate scene  
2. Score every potential laugh moment 1–10 (using Deep-Dive rubric)  
3. Ensure no 3 consecutive scores below 6  

**Revision Passes**:  
- Pass 1: Structure & escalation  
- Pass 2: Joke density & specificity  
- Pass 3: Character truth & heart  
- Pass 4: Rhythm & silence  
- Pass 5: Cultural & tonal polish

- Horror-Comedy: Fear → Relief cycle  
- Action-Comedy: Stunts as physical gags with personality

- Drama-Comedy: Use dramatic sincerity as subversion  

**Fusion Rule**: One genre must always be dominant.

## Master Validation Checklists

### Ultimate Comedy Integrity Checklist
- [ ] Every character has a distinct comedic energy type and verbal tic  
- [ ] Running gags follow full lifecycle  
- [ ] No sequence exceeds 12 minutes without a breather  
- [ ] Minimum 3 voices per major comedic beat  
- [ ] All world rules documented and respected  
- [ ] Laugh density increases through Act 2, resolves with heart in Act 3  
- [ ] Subgenre conventions both honored and subverted at least once  
- [ ] Read-aloud test: minimum 6 strong laughs per 10 pages  
- [ ] Every joke traceable to character, world, or premise  

### AI Generation Quality Gate
- [ ] Internal joke scoring average ≥ 7.5/10 (≥36/50 on Deep-Dive rubric)  
- [ ] Tag-team dialogue ratio ≥ 70%  
- [ ] Environmental elements contribute to at least 30% of laughs  
- [ ] Emotional valleys present between every two peaks
