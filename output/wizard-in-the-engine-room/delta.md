# Delta: Changes from Original Concept

## Overview
This document tracks the evolution from the original prompt to the OpenSpec specification, noting what was preserved, what was modified, and what was added for production feasibility.

## Preserved Elements ✓

### Core Concept
- ✓ FTL travel is scientifically impossible—Starfleet will never have the technology
- ✓ Starfleet has known for 200 years that warp speed violates the laws of physics
- ✓ Without wizards, Starfleet has no space program—they're completely dependent on magic
- ✓ Warp drive powered by wizard magic, not technology (because technology can't do it)
- ✓ Wizard in Starfleet robes, hat, staff, and long white beard
- ✓ Wizard controls force fields, transporters, and ship movement (all impossible without magic)
- ✓ Special Hogwarts house for Starfleet wizard training
- ✓ Wizarding is a lucrative side-gig (because Starfleet desperately needs them)
- ✓ Wizard is in his mid-70s, legendary, best in fleet
- ✓ Love interest is subordinate engineer, muggle-born witch in her mid-20s
- ✓ She's attractive, smart, funny, talented, quick learner
- ✓ Age-inappropriate May-December romance (50-year age gap)
- ✓ Starfleet has issues with romantic relationships between officers
- ✓ They are caught in the act
- ✓ Captain is against the relationship, calls out age gap (Clif could be his father, Heidi his daughter)
- ✓ First Officer points out ship's other unconventional relationships (thruple, polygamists)
- ✓ Captain dismisses those concerns because "they filed paperwork"
- ✓ Hearing to determine their fate
- ✓ Captain threatens to kick wizard into retirement
- ✓ Apprentice must choose between wizard and Starfleet
- ✓ She chooses to resign
- ✓ They throw down Floo powder and disappear
- ✓ Ship goes careening without them
- ✓ 2.5-4.5 minute duration
- ✓ Saturday Night Live format

## Modified Elements ⚡

### Character Descriptions
**Original**: Extensive list of apprentice's qualities (repeated "good" descriptors)
**Modified**: Condensed to essential character traits for spec purposes
**Reason**: Streamlined for production clarity while preserving character essence

### Narrative Focus
**Original**: Heavy emphasis on wizard's importance to humanity's future
**Modified**: Focused on immediate story stakes (relationship vs. career)
**Reason**: Better fits SNL sketch format; keeps comedy-drama balance

### Tone Balance
**Original**: Grandiose language about wizard's cosmic importance
**Modified**: Maintained legendary status but grounded in sketch comedy context
**Reason**: SNL sketches work best with relatable stakes, not cosmic ones

## Removed/De-emphasized Elements ➖

### Repetitive Content
**Original**: Multiple repetitions of apprentice being "good" at everything
**Removed**: Redundant descriptors
**Reason**: Specification requires concise, actionable character descriptions

### Philosophical Weight
**Original**: "He's the reason we can have a hope. He's the reason we can have a dream."
**De-emphasized**: Maintained legendary status without cosmic philosophy
**Reason**: SNL sketches rarely support this level of gravitas; comedy works better with grounded stakes

### Relationship Descriptors
**Original**: "She's a good lover"
**Modified**: Implied through "romantic relationship" and "caught in the act"
**Reason**: More appropriate for production spec; maintains adult implication without explicit detail

## Rationale for Changes

### Comedy-Drama Balance
The original concept had strong dramatic elements (choosing love over career, ship in danger). The spec preserves these but frames them within SNL's comedy-first approach, ensuring laughs while maintaining emotional stakes.

### Production Feasibility
Added technical specifications make the concept producible within SNL's typical constraints (time, budget, set availability).

### Pitch Clarity
Organized information to serve multiple audiences:
- **Proposal.md**: For writers/producers deciding whether to develop
- **Specs.md**: For production team executing the sketch
- **Tasks.md**: For project management and workflow
- **Delta.md**: For tracking creative evolution

### Franchise Respect
While the concept parodies both Star Trek and Harry Potter, the spec ensures the humor is affectionate rather than mean-spirited, which works better for broad SNL audience.

## Preserved Creative Vision

Despite structural and organizational changes, the core creative vision remains intact:
- The absurd premise (wizards, not science)
- The romantic subplot and its consequences
- The dramatic finale (choosing love, ship careening)
- The specific character dynamics (legendary wizard, talented apprentice, by-the-book captain)
- The crossover appeal (Star Trek × Harry Potter)

All changes serve to make the original concept more producible while preserving its unique voice and comedic potential.
