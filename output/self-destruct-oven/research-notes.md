# SNL Format Research Notes

## Key Findings for Self-Destruct Oven Sketch

### Standard SNL Sketch Format

#### Runtime to Page Count Ratio
- **Standard ratio**: 1 page ≈ 1 minute of performance
- **Target**: 2.5-4 minutes = 2.5-4 pages
- SNL sketches typically run 3-5 minutes for non-cold open sketches
- Corporate satire sketches tend toward the shorter end (3-4 minutes)

#### Structure Patterns
1. **Cold Open** (0-30 seconds)
   - Establish setting immediately
   - Visual gag or strong opening line
   - No lengthy exposition

2. **Setup** (30-60 seconds)
   - Introduce premise quickly
   - Establish "game" of the sketch (the central absurd concept)
   - Characters reveal personalities through action, not description

3. **Escalation** (90-150 seconds)
   - Heighten the absurdity progressively
   - Each beat should be more ridiculous than the last
   - Callbacks to earlier jokes
   - Physical comedy opportunities

4. **Button/Ending** (15-30 seconds)
   - Quick, punchy ending
   - Often a twist or reversal
   - Sometimes a callback to opening
   - Avoid explaining the joke

### Corporate Satire Sketch Characteristics

#### Common Elements
- **Boardroom/office setting** - minimal set requirements
- **Jargon overload** - buzzwords used absurdly
- **Yes-men dynamics** - hierarchy comedy
- **Euphemism escalation** - calling bad things by good names
- **Oblivious executives** - disconnected from reality

#### Successful Examples (General Patterns)
- "Wells for Boys" - absurd product presented seriously
- "Papyrus" - obsessive attention to minor detail
- Corporate training videos - deadpan delivery of insane content

### Pacing Notes

#### Dialogue Rhythm
- **Rapid-fire exchanges** in escalation section
- **Overlapping dialogue** for chaos moments
- **Pregnant pauses** for reaction shots
- **Catchphrase repetition** for callback laughs

#### Beat Timing
- Major laugh every 20-30 seconds
- Build to bigger laughs (don't peak too early)
- Final 30 seconds should have the biggest laugh + button

### Character Archetypes for Corporate Sketches

1. **The Spin Doctor** - makes everything sound positive
2. **The Yes-Man** - agrees with everything, no matter how insane
3. **The Whistleblower** - tries to inject sanity, gets ignored
4. **The Oblivious Executive** - completely disconnected
5. **The Jargon Machine** - speaks only in buzzwords
6. **The Nervous Subordinate** - knows it's wrong, too scared to speak

### Stage Directions Best Practices

- **Minimal but specific** - "KAREN slams folder on table" not "Karen expresses frustration"
- **Actor-friendly** - clear blocking that works on SNL's stage
- **Camera-aware** - note key reaction shots
- **Prop-light** - SNL has limited rehearsal time

### Format Specifications

```
TITLE IN CAPS
Setting description (1-2 lines max)

CHARACTER NAMES IN CAPS when speaking
(Parenthetical action/tone notes)
Dialogue in standard case.

[Stage directions in brackets or ALL CAPS]
```

### Comedy Devices for This Sketch

1. **Euphemism Escalation**
   - "Self-cleaning" → "Thermal reset" → "Controlled combustion event"
   
2. **Data Manipulation**
   - "Only 40% of houses burned down completely"
   - "Customer satisfaction is high among survivors"

3. **Blame Shifting**
   - "User error - they should have removed flammable items"
   - "The house was the problem, not the oven"

4. **Marketing Spin**
   - "It's a feature that encourages home renovation"
   - "Creates jobs for firefighters and contractors"

5. **Legal Doublespeak**
   - "Alleged incidents" vs "proven fires"
   - "We're not saying it's safe, we're saying it's not unsafe"

### Key Takeaways for Self-Destruct Oven

✓ Keep it to 3-4 pages maximum
✓ Establish the fire problem within first 30 seconds
✓ Escalate the absurdity of justifications
✓ Use corporate jargon satirically
✓ Each character should have distinct voice/accent
✓ Build to biggest laugh at the end
✓ Physical comedy: maybe someone's phone catches fire during meeting
✓ End with surprising twist that reframes everything

### Timing Breakdown for Our Sketch

- **0:00-0:30** - Cold open, establish boardroom, hint at problem
- **0:30-1:00** - Reveal the fire issue, present "feature vs bug" debate
- **1:00-2:30** - Escalating arguments, corporate spin intensifies
- **2:30-3:30** - Peak absurdity, possible physical gag
- **3:30-4:00** - Twist ending, final button

### Production Notes

- **Set**: Conference table, chairs, maybe a presentation screen
- **Props**: Folders, coffee cups, maybe a small fire extinguisher (for gag)
- **Costumes**: Business attire (suits, blazers)
- **Special considerations**: None - all verbal comedy with minimal effects
